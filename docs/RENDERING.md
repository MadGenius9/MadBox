# MadBox rendering

`WorldRenderer` owns the PixiJS application and renders a serializable `World` supplied by the simulation. Coordinates passed to its public methods are in tiles; each tile is rendered at 16 internal pixels. The main loop supplies elapsed seconds to `render()`.

Terrain is rasterized into a cached canvas texture. The seeded coordinate hash supplies visual variation without advancing the simulation random generator. A second vegetation pass draws tree crowns and contact shadows. Coherent color variation keeps ocean bathymetry smooth. Mountains use continuous slope shading, elevation contours, and spaced large peaks; vegetation positions are jittered to avoid a repeated icon grid. Beaches, shallows, and elevation highlights give the world depth without external image requests. Call `invalidateTerrain()` after terrain or vegetation changes. Replacing the world invalidates this cache automatically, destroys cached creature/building instances (IDs may be reused), and fits the camera. Every 30 simulation ticks, throttled to at most once per two wall-clock seconds, a bounded terrain signature check also detects simulation-driven biome and vegetation-threshold changes, such as forest burned into grass. Unchanged signatures avoid full texture rebuilds. The canvas and GPU texture are reused for refreshes of the same world dimensions. Explicit painting invalidates immediately and bypasses the cosmetic refresh throttle.

Creatures and buildings use reusable generated textures. Creature placement, animation, damage tint, and depth sorting update independently of the cached terrain. Territory visualization has its own layer; it refreshes every 50 simulation ticks when enabled. Active fire locations are cached per simulation tick or explicit edit. Short-lived power particles use a final effects layer; sea glints reuse sprite instances instead of rebuilding geometry. Static atmospheric lighting is baked into the terrain texture to avoid an extra full-screen blend pass. Power feedback includes a branched lightning beam, a meteor streak and expanding impact rings, a rising blessing halo, earthquake ripples and cracks, and falling rain streaks. These overlays are capped at 24 concurrent transients; particles are capped at 1,200. Visual particles intentionally use nondeterministic random numbers and never mutate the world.

Right/middle drag pans; the wheel zooms around the pointer. `panMode=true` enables primary-button and single-touch camera movement for the Inspect tool. Two fingers pan and pinch regardless of the selected tool; once a two-finger gesture begins it consumes movement until all fingers are lifted. `isNavigating` allows the input controller to suppress painting during camera gestures. Brush editing remains the main controller's responsibility.

Use `resetCamera()` to recover the world after large pans. Resize preserves the current world center, and crossing the 600px mobile breakpoint refits the world. Framebuffer multisample antialiasing is disabled because the generated textures already contain smooth edges. Resolution starts at at most 1.5 device pixels and adapts down to 0.65 only under sustained slow frame rates; DOM controls and text remain at native resolution. Detail recovers when sustained performance allows. Sprite textures are shared across all entities. Terrain is rebuilt only after explicit invalidation or a changed visual signature and rendering never creates DOM elements per entity.
